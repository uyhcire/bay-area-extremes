How this was built

  • Income and its uses come from the BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey, {{YEARS}} two-year average, for the San Francisco–Oakland–Hayward metro (San Francisco, San Mateo, Alameda, Contra Costa and Marin counties; San Jose is a separate metro the survey does not publish) and for all U.S. consumer units. A consumer unit is roughly a household.
  • Real dollars divide each line by the matching BEA regional price parity for the San Francisco–Oakland–Fremont metro, averaged over the same two years: housing for shelter, utilities for utilities, goods for groceries, vehicles, fuel, clothing and furnishings, other services for the rest of consumption, and all items for taxes, saving and giving. U.S. lines are divided by the U.S. parities, which sit within about 1.5 points of 100.
  • Taxes are income before taxes minus income after taxes. Net saving is what remains of after-tax income once every expenditure line is paid, so it includes mortgage principal and absorbs the survey's reporting error on both income and spending. Treat it as the least precise branch.
  • Shelter for owners counts mortgage interest, property tax, insurance and upkeep, not the purchase price of the home. Deflating it by the rent-based housing parity is the standard approximation.
  • Real income here is the sum of the deflated branches, so it differs slightly from nominal income divided by the all-items parity.
